## Voice

**Read this block before writing a line of this character's dialogue, or — for a viewpoint
character — a line of their narration.** It is maintained from the manuscript as they speak.
Invented in advance and never updated, it is worse than useless: it will describe someone the
book stopped writing three chapters ago.

### Register

Vocabulary level, formality, sentence length, rhythm. How they sound on an ordinary day **and**
how they sound under pressure — for most characters these are different, and the difference is
the characterization.

### Linguistic habits

Concrete and checkable. The test: if you deleted the habit, would a reader notice something
missing? If not, it isn't a habit, it's a description.

|---|---|---|

### Catchphrases and repeated lines

Verbatim, not paraphrased. **Note when someone else starts saying it** — borrowed speech is one
of the cheapest and strongest relationship signals available.

|---|---|---|

### Never says

The negative space, and it does more work than the habits. Words this character avoids, subjects
they route around, registers they will not use. Give the reason where there is one.

### Under pressure

What breaks first. Most people lose exactly one thing — precision, volume, politeness, grammar,
an accent they've been suppressing, the ability to finish a sentence.

## Personality

### Core traits

Three to five. Each needs a **behaviour that demonstrates it**, not an adjective. "Loyal" is not
a trait; "will not leave a room until everyone he came with has left it" is.

### Wants, needs, won't admit

- **Wants** — concrete, stateable in one sentence, and visible on the page
- **Needs** — what the arc is actually about. Usually not the same thing
- **Won't admit** — to themselves, or to anyone else
