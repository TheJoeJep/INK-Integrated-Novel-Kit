# Credits and Attribution

## Brandon Sanderson

The craft knowledge in `craft/` is derived from **Brandon Sanderson's** publicly published
writing lectures and lecture notes. He has taught creative writing at Brigham Young University
for years and has made the material freely available — on YouTube, as a podcast, and as written
lecture notes on his own website — expressly so that writers can learn from it.

**This system would not exist without that generosity, and the craft in it is his, not ours.**

Where a technique, term, or framework in `craft/` originates with him, the document says so and
links the specific lecture. Terms like *promise / progress / payoff*, *Sanderson's Laws of
Magic*, *worldbuilder's disease*, *iconic characters*, and the *proactive / relatable / capable*
axes are his.

### What this project does and does not contain

This is **not** a copy of his lectures. It contains our own operational rewrites — a technique
restated as when to reach for it, how to apply it, and how to tell whether you got it wrong.
Lecture prose is discursive by nature; a working checklist is not. No transcript or lecture text
is reproduced here.

**If this material is useful to you, go to the source.** It is better, it is free, and it is
his:

- **Lectures on YouTube** — [2025 course playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLSH_xM-KC3ZvzkfVo_Dls0B5GiE2oMcLY)
- **Written lecture notes** — [brandonsanderson.com, 2025 lecture notes](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/tagged/2025-lecture-notes)
- **His website** — [brandonsanderson.com](https://www.brandonsanderson.com)
- **Writing Excuses** — the podcast he co-hosts, [writingexcuses.com](https://writingexcuses.com)

Buy his books. Take the material seriously enough to go read the original.

## Mary Robinette Kowal

**[craft/short-fiction/short-fiction.md](craft/short-fiction/short-fiction.md) is hers, not
Sanderson's.** It derives from her guest lecture on short fiction in the 2020 BYU course — the
MICE Quotient taught in full, the nesting discipline, the length formula, and the four-way
consequence matrix.

Sanderson's role in that lecture is introducing her and doing the exercise alongside the students.
No craft claim in that document is his.

## Brandon Mull

The middle-grade and YA material derives from **Brandon Mull's** guest lecture in the 2016 BYU
course — age-category conventions for romance, violence and language, and his "category fit plus a
cool difference" pitch test.

**Sanderson's own lectures cover none of this.** Mull is the sole source for it here.

- Author of *Fablehaven*, *Beyonders*, and the *Five Kingdoms* series

## Dave Wolverton (David Farland)

**Sanderson credits him repeatedly**, and a surprising amount of what reads as "Sanderson's
teaching" traces back to him:

- **Try-fail cycles** — taught to Sanderson in Wolverton's class
- **"Prose as a pane of glass"** — the framing behind the Box model
- **"Braiding roses"** — the relationship-plot metaphor
- **Dialogue tag mechanics**

Sanderson also says Wolverton's was the **only** class he took that ever taught how to build a
compelling character.

## What is ours

The folder architecture, the skills, the templates, the loop design, the diagnostic index, and
the operational rewrites of the craft material. Those are this project's contribution and are
offered freely.

## A note on what this is for

This system is built to help a writer write — to teach the vocabulary, run the checks, and hold
the documentation, so the author does the work with better tools. It is not built to write books
for people. That distinction is the point of the project, and it is also, as far as we read him,
the spirit of why Sanderson published the lectures in the first place.
