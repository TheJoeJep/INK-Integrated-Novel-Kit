# Glossary

Terms used across the craft base. Sanderson's terms are marked **[BS]**; others are attributed.

---

**Architect** **[BS]** — a writer who plans and outlines before drafting. Opposite of *gardener*.
Most writers are hybrids, in ratios that vary by project.
→ [philosophy-and-practice.md](../process/philosophy-and-practice.md)

**Atomic idea** **[BS]** — a single uncombined fragment kept in a notebook, valuable because it
can combine with anything. Distinct from a premise, which is already committed.
→ [idea-generation.md](../process/idea-generation.md)

**Big P Plot** **[BS]** — what the book is about. The answer to "what's your book about?"
→ [promise-progress-payoff.md](../plot/promise-progress-payoff.md)

**Chef vs. cook** **[BS]** — a chef understands the principles and can invent and troubleshoot;
a cook follows recipes and is stuck when they fail. The governing metaphor of his teaching.
→ [philosophy-and-practice.md](../process/philosophy-and-practice.md)

**Cold open** **[BS]** — opening mid-action with a sequence that is a microcosm of the whole
story. *Raiders of the Lost Ark*'s temple scene.
→ [scene-craft.md](../scene/scene-craft.md)

**Diagnostic habit** — the five questions to ask of any craft rule: what expectation does it
create, what response does it produce, what goes wrong without it, when would breaking it help,
what would you provide instead. The meta-tool for the whole base.
→ [philosophy-and-practice.md](../process/philosophy-and-practice.md)

**Earning out** — when a book's royalties exceed its advance and further payments begin.
→ [publishing.md](../publishing/publishing.md)

**First Law of Magic** **[BS]** — *understanding equals magic.* The ability to solve conflict with
magic is proportional to how well the reader understands it.
→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Gandalf Promise** — a promise kept exactly, timed so the reader has been driven to forget it.
Outperforms most twists. Named for Helm's Deep.
→ [payoff-and-twists.md](../plot/payoff-and-twists.md)

**Gardener** **[BS]** — a discovery writer, who finds the story by writing it. Opposite of
*architect*. Term borrowed from wider writing culture.
→ [philosophy-and-practice.md](../process/philosophy-and-practice.md)

**Hanging a lantern** — acknowledging a narrative oddity inside the story, so the reader trusts
the writer is in control. The gorilla in the phone booth.
→ [scene-craft.md](../scene/scene-craft.md)

**Hard magic** **[BS]** — a system with clear, defined rules the reader understands. Can solve
problems satisfyingly.
→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Iceberg** — the principle that the writer must know far more than the reader sees, in both
worldbuilding and character.
→ [worldbuilding-fundamentals.md](../worldbuilding/worldbuilding-fundamentals.md)

**Iconic character** **[BS]** — a character without an arc, who doesn't change from story to
story. Defined by stories written to be read out of order. Bond, Holmes, Mary Poppins.
→ [arcs-and-iconic-characters.md](../character/arcs-and-iconic-characters.md)

**little p plot** **[BS]** — the chapter-by-chapter problems that accumulate into the Big P Plot.
→ [promise-progress-payoff.md](../plot/promise-progress-payoff.md)

**Maid and butler dialogue** — characters explaining to each other what they both already know.
Named for the stage convention.
→ [prose-and-exposition.md](../prose/prose-and-exposition.md)

**Major Dramatic Question** — the central yes/no question the story answers. Should be clear by
the end of Act One.
→ [structure-models.md](../structure/structure-models.md)

**Michael Problem** **[BS]** — when a character's primary motivation becomes their only
characteristic. Named for the *LOST* character.
→ [proactive-relatable-capable.md](../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md)

**MICE Quotient** — Orson Scott Card's framework (Milieu, Idea, Character, Event). Referenced but
not taught in the sources here.

**Payoff** **[BS]** — a surprising but fulfilling answer to a promise. Both halves required.
→ [payoff-and-twists.md](../plot/payoff-and-twists.md)

**Prescriptive vs. descriptive critique** — a prescription rewrites the author's book; a
description reports the critic's experience. Descriptive is more useful and preserves ownership.
→ [feedback-and-critique.md](../process/feedback-and-critique.md)

**Progress** **[BS]** — the reader's sense that the story is advancing toward its promise. Not the
same as events occurring.
→ [progress-and-signposting.md](../plot/progress-and-signposting.md)

**Promise** **[BS]** — an expectation the opening sets, deliberately or not. Five kinds: tone,
story, character/conflict, structural, plus the cold open as a compound.
→ [promises.md](../plot/promises.md)

**Pyramid of abstraction** **[BS]** — concrete sensory grounding at the base earns abstract
discussion at the apex. Start abstract and the reader has no footing.
→ [prose-and-exposition.md](../prose/prose-and-exposition.md)

**Revision bias** — the failure mode your drafting style predicts. Outliners revise too little;
discovery writers revise too much.
→ [philosophy-and-practice.md](../process/philosophy-and-practice.md)

**Save the Cat** — Blake Snyder's principle: show a character helping someone, and the reader
bonds. Used as a relatability technique.
→ [proactive-relatable-capable.md](../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md)

**Second Law of Magic** **[BS]** — *limitations are more interesting than powers.* Superman is
interesting because of his ethics and kryptonite.
→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Signposting** **[BS]** — narrative markers telling the reader they're making progress. Their
absence is a leading cause of readers abandoning books.
→ [progress-and-signposting.md](../plot/progress-and-signposting.md)

**Soft magic** **[BS]** — a system whose rules are deliberately obscure. Creates wonder; should
complicate problems rather than solve them.
→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Split personality** **[BS]** — the artist drafts, the businessperson revises, and they must not
be in the room together.
→ [revision-and-diagnosis.md](../revision/revision-and-diagnosis.md)

**Stealth thesis paragraph** **[BS]** — opening a chapter by establishing its subject, closing on
resolution or forward pressure. Orients without announcing.
→ [progress-and-signposting.md](../plot/progress-and-signposting.md)

**Story Circle** — Dan Harmon's five-stage structure. Sanderson's preferred model over strict
Campbell.
→ [structure-models.md](../structure/structure-models.md)

**Strange attractor** **[BS]** — combining familiar ideas unexpectedly so the story feels familiar
and strange at once. *This meets this.*
→ [idea-generation.md](../process/idea-generation.md)

**Third Law of Magic** **[BS]** — *expand what you have before you add something new.* Depth over
breadth.
→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Try-fail cycle** — repeated escalating failure before success. The engine most discovery writers
use to finish.
→ [try-fail-cycles.md](../structure/try-fail-cycles.md)

**Villain Problem** **[BS]** — antagonists are naturally more proactive than protagonists, and
quietly become more interesting.
→ [proactive-relatable-capable.md](../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md)

**Viewpoint cluster** **[BS]** — a group of characters whose storylines are interconnected enough
to plan as a single viewpoint entity.
→ [arcs-and-iconic-characters.md](../character/arcs-and-iconic-characters.md)

**Worldbuilder's disease** **[BS]** — paralysis from the scope of worldbuilding; building forever
instead of writing. Also used for systems with breadth but no depth.
→ [worldbuilding-fundamentals.md](../worldbuilding/worldbuilding-fundamentals.md)

**Yes-but / No-and** — scene endings that generate the next scene. *Yes, but* a complication
arrives; *no, and* things get worse.
→ [try-fail-cycles.md](../structure/try-fail-cycles.md)
