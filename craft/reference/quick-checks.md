# Quick Checks

Condensed checklists for use while drafting or revising. Each links to the full treatment.

---

## Before drafting a scene

- [ ] What does this scene progress? Name one thing
- [ ] Whose viewpoint, and what do they want in it?
- [ ] What does the opening establish about the scene's subject?
- [ ] How will it end — **yes-but** or **no-and**?
- [ ] Is the setting filtered through a specific perception?

→ [scene-craft.md](../scene/scene-craft.md)

---

## After drafting a scene

- [ ] Did anything change? If the situation is identical, why is this here?
- [ ] Can the reader *see* what progressed?
- [ ] Anything odd that needs a lantern hung on it?
- [ ] Any exposition sitting undissolved?
- [ ] Any maid-and-butler dialogue?

---

## Opening a book

- [ ] Tone demonstrated within two pages
- [ ] Story promise present — the reader knows the direction
- [ ] Protagonist wants something concrete and is obstructed
- [ ] Genre identifiable
- [ ] No two promises contradict
- [ ] Every promise made is one the book can pay
- [ ] A proactive moment banked, however small

→ [promises.md](../plot/promises.md)

---

## New character

- [ ] What do they want? One concrete sentence
- [ ] What don't they want anyone to know?
- [ ] What failure will they never repeat?
- [ ] Position on each axis — proactive, relatable, capable
- [ ] Which axis is lowest? *That's the arc*
- [ ] Arc or iconic — decided deliberately?
- [ ] Flaws, restrictions, and limitations distinguished?
- [ ] Non-obvious expression of the motivation chosen?
- [ ] Would they survive the three-character no-tags test?

→ [proactive-relatable-capable.md](../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md) ·
[motivation-personality-values.md](../character/motivation-personality-values.md)

## New magic system or speculative element

- [ ] Soft or hard — decided?
- [ ] Solving problems only with what the reader understands?
- [ ] Limitations, weaknesses, and costs — all three named separately?
- [ ] Does using it cost something they'd rather not pay?
- [ ] Societal consequences extrapolated?
- [ ] Expanding what exists rather than adding new?
- [ ] What's the emotional core — what is it *about*?

→ [sandersons-laws-of-magic.md](../worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

---

## Chapter review

- [ ] One thing progressed, and visibly
- [ ] Opens by establishing its subject
- [ ] Closes on resolution or forward pressure
- [ ] Ends yes-but or no-and
- [ ] Contributes to at least one promise
- [ ] Pacing differs from the chapter before it

→ [progress-and-signposting.md](../plot/progress-and-signposting.md)

---

## Act review

- [ ] Any stretch of three-plus chapters without a signpost?
- [ ] Does each beat cost more than the last?
- [ ] Is escalation in kind, or only in scale?
- [ ] Try-fail cycles present — at least two failures before anything important?
- [ ] Does at least one failure come from an internal flaw?
- [ ] Is there a real midpoint pivot?
- [ ] Does every subplot touch the Major Dramatic Question?

---

## Before writing the ending

- [ ] What promise does this answer? Is it the one the book made?
- [ ] Surprising *and* inevitable?
- [ ] Where does the reader genuinely doubt?
- [ ] Does the climax resolve the core thread?
- [ ] Would a kept promise, well-timed, beat the twist? (Gandalf test)
- [ ] Does it cost the protagonist something they'd have refused in Act One?

→ [payoff-and-twists.md](../plot/payoff-and-twists.md)

## Manuscript diagnosis

Three questions first, always:

1. **What did this promise, and did it pay?**
2. **What does this character want, and does it show?**
3. **What progressed here, and can the reader see it?**

Then → [problem-to-technique.md](problem-to-technique.md)

## Starting a revision

- [ ] Which mode are you in — artist or business? Declare it
- [ ] Do you know your revision bias, and are you correcting for it?
- [ ] Have you built a **queue**, or are you about to "fix the novel"?
- [ ] Is the queue ordered structure-first, prose-last?

**The standard order:**
1. Protagonist's motivation
2. Redundant scenes — cut or combine
3. Midpoint reversal
4. Seed what the climax needs
5. Line-level prose — last

→ [revision-and-diagnosis.md](../revision/revision-and-diagnosis.md)

## Reading beta feedback

Sort every note into one of three tiers before acting on any of it:

| | Authority | Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Reaction** — "I got bored here" | High | Believe it |
| **Diagnosis** — "because nothing happens" | Medium | Treat as one hypothesis |
| **Prescription** — "add a fight scene" | Low | Note it, then diagnose yourself |

- [ ] Where did multiple readers react the same way independently?
- [ ] Are you acting on prescriptions instead of locations?

→ [feedback-and-critique.md](../process/feedback-and-critique.md)

## Readiness — is this a novel yet?

- [ ] Two solid ideas in **setting**
- [ ] Two solid ideas in **character**
- [ ] Two solid ideas in **plot**
- [ ] Can you state it as *this meets this*?
- [ ] Can you state the Major Dramatic Question as a yes/no?
