# The Box, and Bracketing

**Source:** Sanderson, 2016 Lectures 5 and 10 · **Tier:** PRIMARY (full transcript)
**Both models are dropped material** — neither appears in the 2020 or 2025 courses. These
transcripts are the only record.

---

## The Box is not a plot structure

Worth stating plainly, because the name misleads: **this is a model of the reader's frame**, not a
story shape.

The prior week taught the **what** — plot, setting, character, tied together by conflict. The Box
teaches the **how**.

He draws a box containing three circles — plot, setting, character — bound by conflict. **The box
itself is the reader's viewing frame.**

The metaphor is Orwell's: **prose as a pane of glass.** You look *through* it at the story. Note
he credits this to **Dave Wolverton**, his teacher, rather than claiming it.

### What's in the box

The frame is made of two choices, and everything else follows from them:

1. **Viewpoint** — person; omniscient vs. limited; and **four first-person subtypes**: memoir,
   epistolary, cinematic, and a first-person/omniscient hybrid
2. **Tense**

Then the downstream craft rules: attention-follows-character, passive voice, hanging a lantern,
and the third-limited secret-identity cheat.

**See [../viewpoint/viewpoint.md](../viewpoint/viewpoint.md)** — the 2020 lecture develops these
subtypes further and is the better source for the modes themselves. The Box's contribution is the
*framing*: viewpoint and tense are not two decisions among many, they are **the glass the reader
looks through**, and they condition everything else.

He presents it explicitly as a **no-right-answers toolkit**, and is openly polemical against
prescriptive teaching.

# Bracketing

**From 2016 Lecture 10, and the stronger find of the two.**

A technique for tracking plot threads: **nested, non-sequential open/close tracking.** You open a
thread, you may open others inside it, and each must be closed — and the order matters.

He works it fully through **Ender's Game**.

## The convergence worth noticing

**This is the same structural insight as Mary Robinette Kowal's MICE nesting** — see
[../short-fiction/short-fiction.md](../short-fiction/short-fiction.md).

Two different teachers, four years apart, in different courses, arriving at the same discipline:
**threads open and close, they nest, and the nesting order is not optional.** Kowal formalizes it
by thread *type* (Milieu, Inquiry, Character, Event) with matching open/close conditions;
Sanderson tracks it structurally without the taxonomy.

**Neither lecture references the other.** That they converge independently is the strongest
evidence in this craft base that the underlying principle is real rather than a personal
preference — and it's the single most useful thing to take from either.

**Practically:** use Kowal's four types to *identify* your threads, and bracketing to *track* them
across a longer work than a short story.

## Two self-critical examples

**Sazed in *The Hero of Ages*.** Seven boring chapters, fixed in about a week by **restructuring
despair as elimination-progress** — the character works through possibilities rather than simply
sinking.

**Note this is the same fix as the Mistborn depression rewrite** in
[../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md](../character/proactive-relatable-capable.md), which
he describes in 2025 without naming the book. Same problem, same solution, told twice across nine
years — which makes *motion is good even if it's downward motion* one of the most load-bearing
rules he teaches.

**The *Way of Kings* four-chapter opening gambit** — his own accounting of what those opening
chapters cost and bought. See [../plot/promise-progress-payoff.md](../plot/promise-progress-payoff.md)
on red marks.

## Checks

- [ ] Have you chosen viewpoint and tense *deliberately*, as the frame rather than as defaults?
- [ ] List every open thread. Is each one closed?
- [ ] Are they closing in **reverse order** of opening?
- [ ] Any thread opened and never closed? That's the commonest cause of an unsatisfying ending
- [ ] For a stalled or despairing character — can the despair be restructured as progress?
