## The MICE Quotient

Four kinds of thread. **Each opens one way and closes a matching way** — the closing condition is
determined by the opening.

| Thread | Opens with | Closes with |
|---|---|---|
| **Milieu** | Entering a place | Exiting it |
| **Inquiry** | A question asked | The question answered |
| **Character** | An identity shift beginning — dissatisfaction with one's own role | Self-definition solidifying |
| **Event** | The status quo disrupted | A new status quo established |

**Diagnostic use:** if a story feels unfinished, check which threads you opened and whether each
got its matching close. A Milieu thread that never exits, or an Inquiry that never answers, reads
as incomplete no matter how satisfying the prose.

**Red herrings, defined precisely:** dead-end Inquiry threads. Which means they still need
closing.

---

## Nesting — the rule that does the work

> **Last opened, first closed.** Like nested function calls.

If you open Milieu and then open Inquiry inside it, **you must close the Inquiry before you can
close the Milieu.**

**Her analogy for the non-programmers:** a labelled box of Milieu toys, with a smaller Inquiry box
inside. You take everything out to play — that's telling the story — and then repack in reverse.
Get the order wrong and you're left holding an extra piece and some unexplained Allen wrenches.

### Worked example — *The Wizard of Oz*

```
Character   Dorothy is dissatisfied with being a Kansas farm girl
  Event       the tornado disrupts the status quo
    Milieu      arrival in Oz
    …           (closes: she leaves Oz)
  …           (closes: new status quo at home)
…             (closes: she knows who she is)
```

Each thread closes in reverse order of opening. That's the whole structure of the film.

## The length formula

> **length ≈ (characters + locations) × 750 words × (number of MICE threads) ÷ 1.5**

Useful in both directions:

- **Forecasting** — how long is this going to be?
- **Cutting** — if you need a shorter piece, the levers are visible: fewer characters, fewer
  locations, or **fewer threads**. Dropping one MICE thread is the biggest single cut available

See [../structure/scale-and-length.md](../structure/scale-and-length.md).

## Structure of a very short story

Her five-stage worked exercise, with proportions:

**1. Opening** — establish **Who / Where / Genre**, and open your threads.

**2. First try-fail cycle** (~5 sentences). Identify which thread the character is working:
crossing a threshold (Milieu), seeking an answer (Inquiry), struggling with themselves
(Character), or changing the status quo (Event). **That tells you what conflict to throw next** —
block the threshold, block the answer, deepen the unhappiness, add another disruption.

**3. Proportion check.** She has the class notice mid-exercise that setup is about the first third,
and the first try-fail cycle already outweighs it.

**4. The turn** — roughly the 2/3 to 3/4 mark. **The story shifts from opening mode to closing
mode.** This is structural, not a mood change.

**5. Ending** (~3 sentences). Close every thread in nested order, and **mirror the opening's
Who / Where / Genre — but show the delta.** Same three slots, changed contents.

## Her four-way consequence matrix

**Fuller than the two-way version taught elsewhere in this base:**

| | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yes, but** | Progress — but pushed back, or it costs something |
| **No, and** | No progress, and things get worse |
| **No, but** | Failure, with a consolation or partial gain |
| **Yes, and** | Progress, and more besides |

**The critical addition:** **Yes-And is the closing-mode signal.** When the character starts
making sustained progress rather than trading progress for setbacks, you have structurally entered
the final movement.

That makes the matrix a **position indicator**, not just a scene-ending tool — you can tell where
you are in a story by which quadrant its beats are landing in.

**And the last cycle flips to try-*succeed*.** See
[../structure/try-fail-cycles.md](../structure/try-fail-cycles.md).

*(She flags her own Yes-And example — defusing a bomb that goes out the window and lands on the
villain — as weak, and keeps it anyway to show the shape.)*

## Compression techniques

**Imply earlier try-fail cycles as having happened off-page.** A single line noting a character had
already isolated part of the problem implies a whole round of work before the first sentence —
buying backstory without spending words dramatizing it.

**Worked example she runs:** the *Star Wars* trash compactor, analysed as a Milieu escape thread.

## Checks

- [ ] Which MICE threads have you opened?
- [ ] Does each have its **matching** close — exit, answer, self-definition, new status quo?
- [ ] Are they closing in **reverse order** of opening?
- [ ] Any red herrings? They're Inquiry threads and still need closing
- [ ] Run the length formula — does the target length match the thread and cast count?
- [ ] If it's too long: which thread can go?
- [ ] Where's the turn, and is it at roughly two-thirds?
- [ ] Have your beats shifted to **Yes-And**? If not, you haven't entered closing mode
- [ ] Does the ending mirror the opening's Who/Where/Genre and show the change?

## Failure modes

- **Unclosed threads.** The most common cause of a short story that "doesn't land"
- **Wrong-order closes.** The extra Allen wrench
- **Mismatched close.** Answering an Inquiry when you opened a Milieu
- **No turn.** The piece stays in opening mode and then simply stops
- **Too many threads for the length.** Run the formula
