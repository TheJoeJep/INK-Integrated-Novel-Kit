# /magic-system — the Laws as an interview

> **Tier 3 — procedural.** The Laws and their tests are Sanderson's. Specific system suggestions
> are mine and marked as raw material.

**Say this on invocation:** *Working the Laws as an interview — the tests are Sanderson's, any
system content I propose is raw material.*

**Source:** [`craft/worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md`](../../craft/worldbuilding/sandersons-laws-of-magic.md)

**Applies beyond magic** — technology, institutions, political systems, any invented capability.

---

## Zeroth Law first — what's the awesome thing?

> Always err on the side of what is awesome.

**Start here, not with mechanics.** His own systems begin with a cool image worked backward —
*fantasy power armour is cool, how do I justify it?*

**Ask:** what's the image or moment this system exists to deliver?

If the answer is a rule set rather than an image, that's worth knowing before going further.

---

## First Law — where on the hard/soft spectrum?

> Your ability to solve problems with magic in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how
> well the reader understands that magic.

**It's a spectrum, and you can run both ends** — Tolkien does. The Ring is nearly hard: invisibility,
lifespan drain, corruption, with **Gollum on the page so the reader sees the endpoint.** Gandalf is
soft: undefined, off-screen, there to make hobbits feel small.

**The operating rule:**

> **Solve** problems with magic the reader understands. **Create** problems with magic they don't.

**Ask:**
- Which parts will the reader understand fully?
- Which stay obscure?
- Which parts will the climax rely on? *(Those must be the understood parts.)*

**The trap, in his own words against himself:** he gave Vin a new power at the 95% mark of
*Mistborn* on an editor's note, and built the First Law out of realizing it afterwards.

**Series tip:** stay mostly hard, but leave **one mystery tool unexplained** in book one.

### The Aragorn test

The clearest check available. Gandalf's return at Helm's Deep and Aragorn's ghost army at Minas
Tirith are structurally identical rescues with opposite effects — and the difference isn't magic
comprehension.

> **You need to understand what was PROMISED, not the mechanics.**

**Ask:** what has been promised to the reader about what this system can do?

## Second Law — limitations, and it's a character principle first

> Flaws or limitations are more interesting than powers.

**He states this about characters before magic.** Superman is interesting because of his ethics
and kryptonite — not the flying. That's why this skill overlaps `/new-character`.

**Name all three, separately:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Flaw** | Fixable through growth or effort |
| **Limitation** | Structural, not meant to be fixed, worked within |
| **Cost** | A price paid per use |

*He renames these in every telling — flaw/handicap/challenge, flaw/restriction/limitation,
flaw/limitation/hindrance/cost. The concepts are stable; ignore the labels.*

**Minimal test:** *you can fly* is a story starter. *You can fly, but only while your parents are
asleep* immediately generates plot.

**Ask:**
- What can it **not** do?
- What does using it **cost**, and would the character rather not pay it?
- Can the reader **see the endpoint** early, Gollum-style?
- Is there a **quirk with no cost** the plot might later lean on? *(Luke never loses at holochess
  — fine, until the plot needs him to win a tournament.)*

---

## Third Law — expand before adding

> Before adding something new, see if you can instead expand what you have.

**Born from his own failure:** the 2002 *Way of Kings* draft — 300,000 words, ten character arcs
each ~10% finished, **thirty planned magic systems.** Chasing bigger. The book failed.

**Three ways to expand:**

- **Extrapolate** — what happens when? How has this changed society, economy, daily life? A
  capability that has existed for centuries **has changed things**, and those changes are free
  worldbuilding
- **Interconnect** — tie it to theme; connect powers to each other
- **Streamline** — consolidate, combine, cut

**Ask:** are you adding a power where you could deepen one you have?

## Rules

- **Zeroth Law first.** A system with no image behind it is engineering, not story
- **Always ask what it can't do** before what it can
- **Extrapolation is the most-skipped step** and the most valuable — press on it
- **Check for a project-specific constraint** in `world/` or `story/` and test every proposal
  against it before offering it
- **Offer options, never one design.** Tier 4 content inside a Tier 3 process
- **Don't write the system into docs unasked** — that's `/write` in Structure Mode
