# /brainstorm — atomic ideas and the strange attractor

> **Tier 4 — generative.** Every idea below is a fragment to react to, reject, or recombine —
> **not a premise, and not a recommendation.** I keep them deliberately unfinished. Finishing one
> into a premise is `/new-story`'s job, done on purpose, later.

**Say this on invocation:** *Generating atomic ideas — these are fragments to react against, not
a premise. I'll keep them small on purpose; tell me if I start finishing one for you.*

**Source:** [`craft/process/idea-generation.md`](../../craft/process/idea-generation.md)

---

## Keep it atomic

**His method:** a notebook of **atomic ideas** — single, small, uncombined fragments. Then look
for combinations that produce something interesting.

Atomic ideas are not premises. They're raw material:

- A visual — cathedral lights in mist
- A structural notion — an *Ocean's Eleven* heist crew, in a fantasy world
- A question, an image, a rule, a character shape

**Why atomic matters:** a fragment can combine with anything. A finished premise can't, because
it's already committed. This is a Zettelkasten-style practice — the value comes from the
collisions between notes, not from any single note being good.

---

## The strange attractor

> **The term isn't Sanderson's.** "Strange attractor" for this move — combining two familiar
> things into one unfamiliar one — belongs to screenwriter Terry Rossio. Sanderson teaches the
> same practice without the label. The label is kept here, attributed, because it names the move
> exactly.

**Combine familiar ideas in unexpected ways**, so the result feels familiar and strange at once.
The shorthand: *it's this meets this.*

**Sanderson's own worked example:** heist + epic fantasy + *My Fair Lady* master/apprentice =
*Mistborn*.

**Why it works:** the familiar half gives the reader footing; the strange half gives them a
reason. Pure novelty is disorienting. Pure familiarity is a book they've already read.

---

## Why combination, not invention

**Plot is the hardest pillar to innovate on. Setting is the easiest.** The strange attractor gets
originality out of *combination* rather than out of inventing a new plot from nothing — the
cheaper and more reliable route.

Same move, different name: stripping a plot to its archetype and re-dressing it in different genre
trappings — see [`craft/plot/plot-archetypes.md`](../../craft/plot/plot-archetypes.md).

**Practical consequence for this skill:** when a combination is thin on plot, don't reach for a
brand-new plot idea. Reach for a second familiar structure to collide with what's already there.

---

## Procedure

**1. Collect atomic ideas.** Ask for what's already sitting around — an image, a rule, a question,
a character shape, a structure borrowed from an unrelated genre. **Push for smallness.** If an
answer arrives already carrying a plot, split it back into fragments before logging it.

**2. Look for collisions.** Offer combinations across ideas already logged — including ones from
different sessions. Present each as *this meets this*, labelled by which half is familiar and
which is strange. **Minimum three combinations per pass.**

**3. Test the mix.** Familiar-and-strange, or just one of the two?
- Pure novelty — no reader footing. Flag it.
- Pure familiarity — competent and forgettable. Flag it.

**4. Stop at the combination.** Don't develop it into a premise here — that's deliberately out of
scope. Note which combination seems worth pursuing and hand off.

## Output format

```
── ATOMIC IDEAS ── logged this session

  1. VISUAL       cathedral lights seen through wet fog, from underneath
  2. STRUCTURE    an Ocean's Eleven heist crew, dropped into a setting with
                  no concept of consent
  3. QUESTION     what if the thing draining a hero's power is also the
                  thing keeping them sane?
  4. RULE         a magic that only works on things you're willing to lose

── COMBINATIONS ── (mine, raw material — this meets this)

  A. #2 × #4      heist crew × a magic paid for in what you love
                  FAMILIAR:  heist structure, competent-crew banter
                  STRANGE:   every job costs a real possession, chosen live
                  ●●●  footing AND reason — worth developing

  B. #1 × #3      cathedral-light visual × sanity-power tradeoff
                  FAMILIAR:  gothic power fantasy
                  STRANGE:   none yet — currently just aesthetic
                  ●○○  pure novelty risk — needs a familiar half

── NOT YET A PREMISE ──
  These are fragments. Take a combination you want to pursue to /new-story
  rather than developing it further here.
```

---

## Rules

- **Keep ideas atomic.** If an answer arrives pre-combined with a plot attached, split it back
  into fragments before logging it
- **Offer multiple combinations, never one.** Label which half is familiar and which is strange on
  every combination offered
- **Don't invent to fill a thin idea.** A thin fragment gets logged as thin, not padded out
- **Don't develop a chosen combination into a premise here** — hand off to `/new-story`
- **Attribute honestly.** Say plainly when a term (like the strange attractor) isn't Sanderson's
