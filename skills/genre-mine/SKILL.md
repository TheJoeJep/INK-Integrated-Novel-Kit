# /genre-mine: build a structure from what you love

> **Tier 3. Procedural.** Stages run in order and none get skipped, especially the slow one.
> Where I suggest a transplant or a beat, that's raw material. The derivation is the process,
> the content is yours.

**Say this on invocation:** *Building a structure from the ground up. Narrowing the genre first.
This one doesn't skip to borrowing, that's the point of it.*

**Source:** [`craft/structure/genre-structure-mining.md`](../../craft/structure/genre-structure-mining.md)

---

## Why this one is different from every other structure skill

`/structure` and `/outline` hand you a shape someone else built. **This is the one you build.**
That's exactly why it trains judgment instead of borrowing it. You understand every beat because
you're the one who derived it, not because someone else's checklist said it belongs there.

> I really like this one simply because it allows you to practise your chef versus cook.

A cook follows the recipe. A chef understands why each step is there and can rebuild it in a
different kitchen. This skill is the exercise, not the shortcut.

---

## Stage 1: Narrow the sub-genre

Not *adventure*. Not even *spy movie* or *heist*. **A specific kind of heist.** Push back if
what's offered is still broad enough to have dozens of unrelated examples living inside it. That's
the single most common way this exercise goes shallow.

**The worked example to hold up, because it's the clearest demonstration available:**

*Hoosiers*, *Miracle*, *Cool Runnings*, *Pitch Perfect*, *Ender's Game*, and **the bridge crew in
*The Way of Kings*** are all the same plot. The underdog sports story: a team that's bad at what
they're supposed to be doing, and a coach or leader who arrives, focuses them, and points them at
something that looks impossible. The stakes vary enormously across that list. Nobody's getting
executed in *Pitch Perfect*. And the shape survives every change in stakes intact. That's the
signature of a genre narrow enough to actually mine.

## Stage 2: Collect examples

Gather everything loved that does this narrow thing, across books, film, and any other medium it
shows up in. **This stage cannot be rushed and shouldn't be pretended past.** Sanderson spent
*years* reading and watching before he could see the whole underdog-sports shape clearly enough to
transplant it into an epic fantasy war camp. A weekend of watching produces a shallower version:
say so if the author is trying to compress this into one sitting, rather than letting the skill
silently produce a thin result.

## Stage 3: Study why each beat works

For every source collected: what's actually great about it here, specifically? And. Just as
important. Which beats *don't* land, even in something loved overall? The question isn't *what
happens*, it's *why does it work when it works*.

**This is the step that separates deriving a structure from copying one.** Lifting beats without
knowing why they land is the self-inflicted version of the exact Lucas-checklist failure named in
`/structure`. The difference is nobody handed you the checklist this time, you wrote it yourself
without doing the work first.

## Stage 4: Build the list of recurring points

Points that show up across more than one source and land when they do. They don't all have to be
present in every example collected. That's expected. What matters is that each one earns its
place by recurring *and* by having a known reason it works.

## Stage 5: Decide what to drop

Name, explicitly, which recurring beats are being cut for this project. This is real information
. A dropped beat is a deliberate choice about what this version of the structure isn't going to do,
and it should be visible, not quietly absent.

## Stage 6: Transplant

Move the derived structure into a different genre, medium, or aesthetic. **Far enough away that it
reads as new**, not as the same story with the names changed. Underdog-sports structure dropped
into an epic-fantasy war camp is why the *Way of Kings* bridge crew doesn't read as derivative even
though every single beat in it is borrowed. Transplanting too close to the source genre is the
failure mode that undoes all the work of the previous five stages.

## Output format

```
── GENRE MINE ── underdog-team structure, for [thread]

  SOURCES        Hoosiers · Miracle · Cool Runnings · Pitch Perfect ·
                 Ender's Game · The Way of Kings (bridge crew)
  NARROW ENOUGH  ✓ team-forms-under-a-coach, not "sports" or "adventure"

  RECURRING BEATS
    1. team introduced failing at the thing it's nominally for      kept. Why: establishes the gap the coach closes
    2. an outsider arrives with a different method                  kept. Why: contrast makes the old method visibly wrong
    3. early method rejected, then tried anyway under pressure      kept. Why: this IS the try-fail cycle at team scale
    4. a personal cost paid by the leader or a member                dropped. No analog in this thread yet
    5. the impossible win, executed with the earned method           kept. Why: the payoff has to use what was taught, not luck

  TRANSPLANT     underdog-sports shape → [target genre/thread]
                 distance from source                                ✓ far enough / ⚠ too close

── VERDICT ── whole structure visible at once. Ready to seed as points on the map

── OPTIONS ── (mine, raw material)
   ...
```

## Rules

- **Don't skip or compress the study stage.** A shallow pass produces vague generalities instead
  of usable beats. Say so if it's being rushed
- **Name why each beat works, not just that it recurs.** "It's in three of five sources" isn't a
  reason on its own
- **Explicitly name what's being dropped**, not just what's kept
- **Transplant far enough away that it reads as new.** Flag it if the target genre is close enough
  to the source that the result will read as derivative
- **This produces a shape, not prose.** Stage 6 output is raw material. Drafting is `/write`
- **Don't invent proper nouns for the transplanted structure.** Naming belongs to the author,
  checked against `canon/names-and-terms.md` if this feeds a specific project
