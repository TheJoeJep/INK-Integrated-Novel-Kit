Plot & Promise32 items · 1 unresolved
Promise / Progress / Payoff
Every thread promises, shows progress, and pays off. Works at book, act, chapter and scene scale.
2025 L2
Big P vs little p plot
Big P = what the book is about. little p = the chapter-by-chapter problems accumulating into it.
2025 L2
Plot, setting, character → conflict
Their intersection IS conflict. A scene where all three are present but not pressing on each other is dead.
2025 L2
Don't innovate on plot
Setting is easiest to innovate, character second, plot hardest — and it mostly doesn't need it. Dune = Hamlet = The Lion King.
2025 L2
Fix the promise, not the progress
When they mismatch, change the opening. The middle is what you actually wanted to write. 3 chapters is cheap; 40 isn't.
2025 L2
Tone promise
Set by jokes, a beheading, character names. Stark is a tone promise. Hogwarts promises whimsy.
2025 L2
The epic fantasy prologue, explained
The plot starts slow; the prologue exists to deliver the tone promise. Near-cliché now — use deliberately.
2025 L2
Cold open = story in microcosm
Raiders does in 15 pages what the film does in 150 — including Indy losing.
2025 L2
Character promise: want / need / obstacle
Three things, established early.
2025 L2
Progress is the most important of the three
And the least showy. "90% of your book."
2025 L2
Give a sense of progress, not progress
You control time absolutely. The job is the reader's *sense* of satisfying advancement.
2025 L2
Find the soul of your story
Guardians is ostensibly the MacGuffin; the real story is friendship, and the climax is taking hands.
2025 L2
Why books get boring
You gave an interesting promise, then got interested in something else. See: Finn and the horses.
2025 L2
The Oathbringer fix
Readers bored by a diversion despite heavy worldbuilding. Fix: one new mid-book promise giving the POV a stake.
2025 L2
Worldbuilding is the least important pillar
Great worldbuilding + bad character/plot = an encyclopedia. Time-turners break Harry Potter; Azkaban is still best.
2025 L2
Thriller pacing dies at ~70k words
Short chapters + hooks works to 90–100k. Past that you lose people.
2025 L2
Payoff = surprising yet inevitable
Both halves required.
2025 L2
Plausible deniability
Make them believe it won't happen. The workhorse — reach for it before a twist.
2025 L2
The gift metaphor for twists
Promise a toy car; make them want a plane; give the plane.
2025 L2
The Gandalf Promise
A promise kept exactly, timed so they've forgotten it. Beats most twists. Helm's Deep vs the ghost army.
2025 L2
You don't need a twist
Pride and Prejudice promises a marriage and delivers one. Complications and escalations matter more.
2025 L2
Killing your darlings = a lie you tell yourself
"I'll find a better place for this." 9 times in 10 you won't — the idea wasn't that good.
2025 L2
Red marks
Every great book has things that make people put it down. Remove the non-integral ones; lean on the rest.
2025 L2
Arcs can go sour
Any archetype inverts. A relationship plot becomes a betrayal arc.
2025 L4.5
Readers follow the POV's attention
If the narrator still trusts someone, the reader discounts the cracks too. The betrayal-arc engine.
2025 L4.5
Bittersweet balance rule
Fail the Big P but grow = sweet. Achieve it at personal cost = bitter. Adjust via the arc, not the outcome.
2025 L4.5
Two heist types
Italian Job: the plan collapses, they reuse what they built. Ocean's Eleven: the plan executes, and an outsider supplies tension.
2025 L4.5
The bashing grab
A third heist type, from Joe Russo on Infinity War — no plan, just force, and the story is consequences.
2020 L3
Three mystery types
Whodunit; cat-and-mouse (the protagonist is the quarry); and the Columbo inversion where the reader knows from the start.
2025 L4.5 · 2020 L3
Archetypes can transition mid-book
Mistborn: heist → revolution. Knives Out: whodunit → cat-and-mouse at the midpoint.
2025 L4.5
Umbrella plot vs core plot
A distinct framing that does NOT map onto Big P / little p.
2020 L2unresolved
Three payoff variants
Straight, expansion, substitution. 2025 compresses these into one.
2020 L2
Structure23 items · 2 unresolved
The obstacle rule
Every obstacle must force, reinforce, or show failure in growth. Otherwise it's an event, not a story beat.
2025 L3
The king-died adage
Conflict and struggle make a story — not the sequence of events.
2025 L3
Progress can be regressive
Breaking Bad is entirely backward progress and works.
2025 L3
Dan Harmon's Story Circle
His preferred model over strict Campbell. Simpler, more breathing room.
2025 L3
The Lucas cautionary tale
The monomyth needs divine parentage, so Anakin gets a virgin birth. He was in the theatre. Structures must stay disposable.
2025 L3
Hero's Journey — a changed position
Taught positively with a full Campbell walkthrough in 2020; only critiqued in 2025.
2020 L2 → 2025 L3unresolved
Three-act is really four
Acts 1 and 3 short; act 2 double length with a big beat in the middle.
2025 L3
Major Dramatic Question
From Joe Russo — his single most important tool. Must be *asked* by the end of act one.
2025 L3
The midpoint
The most important beat. Escalation, reframing, or making it personal. Needn't be a surprise.
2025 L3
Yes-but / No-and
Credited by Sanderson to Mary Robinette Kowal. Every scene ends one way or the other.
2020 L3
The four-way matrix
Adds no-but and yes-and. Yes-And is the closing-mode signal — a position indicator, not just a scene tool.
2020 L7 (Kowal)
Try-fail as reader management
Retire the obvious solution the reader is shouting, or they stop trusting the story.
2025 L3
Fail intelligently
The most intelligent attempt, executed cleverly, failing on a flaw or being out-thought.
2025 L3
Points on the map
Jordan's method — six bullet points, discovery-write between them. He pitched 3 books; it took 14.
2025 L3
Try-succeed cycles
Three substitutes for failure: hidden tragedy (Dune), competence under danger (the trapeze act), scope expansion.
2025 L4.5
Genre structure mining
Pick a narrow sub-genre, study it for years, transplant it. Hoosiers → the Way of Kings bridge crew.
2025 L3
Escalate in kind, not scale
Narrower, costlier, more compromised, more isolated, faster — not just bigger.
2025 L3
120k = readers expect bigger scope
Hard to stay small past that. Scope can be stakes OR breadth of time.
2025 L4.5
Chapter length is a pacing control
Long chapters let readers stop; short ones pull them through. He varies it deliberately.
2025 L4.5
The Box
NOT a plot structure — a model of the reader's frame. Viewpoint and tense as the pane of glass. Credited to Wolverton via Orwell.
2016 L5
Bracketing
Nested, non-sequential thread tracking, worked through Ender's Game. Dropped after 2016.
2016 L10
Bracketing ≡ MICE nesting
Two teachers, four years apart, neither referencing the other, same discipline. Independent convergence.
2016 L10 + 2020 L7
Dan Wells ≠ Dan Harmon
Seven-Point Story Structure vs the Story Circle. Two different Dans.
2016 L3unresolved
Character25 items · 1 unresolved
Proactive / Relatable / Capable
Three axes. Very high on one, moderate on another, growing on the third.
2025 L5
The axes are domain-specific
Not personality traits. Score the character in the situation, not globally.
2025 L5
Proactivity makes a character readable
The single most important element. Jack in Lost was going to die in the pilot until they noticed.
2025 L5
The villain problem
Villains out-proact heroes. Three fixes: accept it, let plot force it, or seed it small early.
2025 L5
Motion is good even if downward
Static is what's hard to write. His Mistborn depression rewrite: still sinking, but working through it point by point.
2025 L5
Relatable ≠ likable
A sharpening he made after 2020, which used "likability" unexamined.
2025 L5
Save the cat / kick the dog
Blake Snyder. Kindness or cruelty to something innocent, instantly.
2025 L5
See them through another's eyes
Way of Kings ch.1 is deliberately not Kaladin's POV. "We like what we like other people liking."
2025 L5
Nudge a villain up on relatability
Magneto's goal isn't relatable; his fear is. Slightly-relatable villains are the strongest.
2025 L5
Relatability is relative
The faster driver is a maniac; the slower one is an idiot. The whole antihero engine.
2025 L5
Hero vs protagonist
Hero does virtuous things. Protagonist is who we root for. Snow Crash winks at it.
2025 L5
Capability isn't combat
Sam Gamgee's superpower is loyalty that won't break — and it saves the world.
2025 L5
Flaw / restriction / limitation
Should-overcome / self-imposed / inflicted. He renames these SIX times across three courses.
2025 L5unresolved
The Michael problem
A strong motivation became the character's only trait. Asked about lunch: "I want my son."
2025 L5
Motivation = 60% of character problems
When a character "doesn't work," check motivation first. Not dialogue, not prose.
2025 L5
Motivation / personality / values
What they want, how they express it, what principles shape both.
2025 L6
Same want, three expressions
A character who loves family: shared meals, extra hours, or teaching self-defence. Pick the non-obvious one.
2025 L6
Characters take several drafts
Three Vins. Two Kaladins. Dalinar worked first time — "too stubborn to be anybody else."
2025 L6
Iconic characters
No arc, and just as valid. Stories written to be read out of order. Bond, Holmes, Mary Poppins.
2025 L6.5
Iconic villains refuse the lesson early
The Joker already decided, before the film. Batman changes; the Joker's stasis is his downfall.
2025 L6.5
The apprentice arc
The friction is where they DIVERGE from the master, not where they follow.
2025 L6.5
Tragic vs redemptive arc
Both reach the moment of knowing. One refuses anyway; one chooses differently.
2025 L6.5
No paint-by-numbers arcs
"Fail three times then succeed" isn't it. What matters is WHY they'd choose differently now.
2025 L6.5
The backslide warning
Don't relearn the same lesson book to book. Spider-Man 2 is the exception — and most won't pull it off.
2025 L6.5
Show the flaw's cost first
You can't pay off a cost you never established. And most flaws are double-edged — hubris is confidence.
2025 L6.5
Viewpoint6 items
Three first-person modes
Epistolary, flashback/classic, cinematic. Each buys and costs something different.
2020 L4
Only flashback licenses "this matters later"
In present-narrator mode the same move reads as authorial intrusion.
2020 L4
Hiding in third limited is cheating
His words, about his own Kelsier reveal. "Sometimes a necessary cheat."
2020 L4
Seed mode-shifts early
Skyward plants third-limited interludes so a late switch doesn't jar. One instance sets the convention.
2020 L4
Favourites polarization is unsolved
Readers pick favourites and skim others. He admits there's no clean fix.
2020 L4
Viewpoints expand scope by distance
Two people on two continents, not two people in a room.
2025 L6
Prose & Dialogue12 items
MICRO — dialogue
Motivation, Individuality, Conflict, Realism, Objective — RANKED. Objective matters least.
2016 L11
New writers optimize the least important element
They write to the Objective. Result: characters reciting the outline.
2016 L11
Dialogue needs tension, not argument
Both parties want something. Sherlock needs Watson's humanity; Watson needs his genius.
2016 L11
"It doesn't feel real" has three causes
Missing motivation, missing individuality, or set-piece speechifying. Don't take the note at face value.
2016 L11
The no-tags exercise
Three characters, no tags, no description. If you can't tell them apart, individuality failed.
2025 L6
Signal intelligence by thought structure
Not vocabulary. Ken Jennings doesn't use twenty-dollar words.
2025 L6
Quirks must express a deeper trait
David is bad at metaphors because he leaps before thinking. The five-star narrator didn't work.
2025 L6
Exposition is spinach in the smoothie
It still has to be in there — suspended in something else.
2025 L4
Pyramid of abstraction
Concrete sensory grounding earns abstract discussion. Describe how magic FEELS before how it works.
2025 L8
Maid and butler dialogue
Characters telling each other what they both know. Find someone who genuinely doesn't know.
2020 L6
Prose has three advantages over film
Interiority, space, and a participatory reader who fills in blanks.
2025 L4.5
Layer internal and external conflict
Don't batch character work. And the one-two punch: the same failure again, bigger, unaffordable.
2025 L4.5
Scene Craft3 items
Gorilla in the phone booth
Anything that makes a reader stop to ask an unsignalled question.
2025 L3
Hanging a lantern
From stage: light the badly-built set piece and have someone remark on it. Use sparingly.
2025 L3
Plot holes = what kicks readers out
Not what a forum can construct later. If beta readers snag, that's the signal.
2025 L3
Worldbuilding & Magic17 items · 3 unresolved
Zeroth Law — err toward awesome
Stories start from a cool image worked backward. Fantasy power armour → how do I justify it?
2020 L5
First Law — understanding equals magic
Solving problems with magic works in proportion to reader understanding. He calls it a law of foreshadowing.
2020 L5 · 2025 L7
The First Law is really about promises
Gandalf's return vs Aragorn's ghosts — same rescue, opposite effect. You need to understand what was PROMISED.
2020 L5
The 95% power
He gave Vin a new power at the climax on an editor's note, and built the First Law out of the mistake.
2020 L5
Hard vs soft magic
A spectrum. Solve with what they understand; create problems with what they don't. Tolkien runs both ends.
2020 L5
Second Law — limitations > powers
Stated as a CHARACTER principle first. Superman is his ethics and kryptonite, not the flying.
2020 L5 · 2025 L7
Flaw / limitation / cost
The magic-side taxonomy. Fixable / structural / paid per use.
2020 L5unresolved
Show the endpoint early
Gollum on the page. The Wheel of Time prologue shows the madness before the story starts.
2020 L5
Third Law — expand, don't add
Born from the failed 2002 Way of Kings: 300k words, ten arcs, thirty magic systems. It failed.
2020 L5
The hollow iceberg
His correction of the standard metaphor. You don't build the mass — you build enough that it LOOKS bottomless.
2020 L5
Worldbuilder's disease
Paralysis by scope. Pick key elements; keep deadlines; expand before adding.
2025 L8
Physical / cultural — or three?
2020 folds magic into PHYSICAL setting. 2025 makes it a third pillar. A real structural change.
2020 L6 vs 2025 L8unresolved
Bonehead characters
Kaladin doesn't know horses because Sanderson doesn't. A characterized gap is forgiven; a faked one isn't.
2025 L9
Research to 50%, then draft
50→75% costs ten times as much. Write, then get expert readers and revise on demand.
2025 L9
Conscious vs accidental
Bridge Four has no women — defensible and deliberate. The all-male Mistborn crew was absorbed and unnoticed.
2025 L9
One error collapses all trust
Get a real-world detail wrong and readers stop trusting your judgment on everything, including character.
2025 L9
The masquerade is NOT covered
Checked across three sources. Nothing in 39 lectures addresses why the world hasn't noticed.

Process & Revision19 items · 1 unresolved
Chef, not cook
Understand why the recipe works. Learn rules well enough to break them on purpose.
2025 L1
Advice is individual, not universal
Two successful writers give opposite advice because they have different problems.
2025 L1
Three to five novels first
Their job is discovering your process, not selling.
2025 L1
The product of your writing time is you
Measure by what you became, not only by pages.
2025 L1
100k words in 4 hrs/week
Over a year. Most people who think they have no time have four hours.
2020 L1
A 15-minute interruption costs 45
Not proportional. Protect the block, not the minutes.
2020 L1
Menial day jobs are better
Programming and teaching use the same resource writing needs.
2020 L1
Odds of going pro — they disagree
1-in-4, 1-in-20, and 1-in-100 across different lectures. Recorded side by side, not averaged.
variousunresolved
Atomic ideas
Keep fragments, not premises. Fragments combine; finished premises don't.
2020 L2
The strange attractor
Credited to Terry Rossio. Familiar + strange. Heist + fantasy + My Fair Lady = Mistborn.
2016 L3
Receive critique in silence
Explaining contaminates the sample. No reader will stand beside your book explaining it.
2025 L1
Discovery writers should delay feedback
Critique before you've told yourself the story lets consensus redirect the book.
2025 L1
The five-stage revision framework
1.0 draft → 2.0 tiered self-revision → alpha → 3.0 → beta → 4.0 → 5.0 polish. The most systematic material in the corpus.
2020 L8
Work the top tier first
Book-spanning problems invalidate everything fixed below them.
2020 L8
Alphas diagnose, betas react
Asking the wrong one for the wrong thing is why feedback rounds go badly.
2020 L8
Test an editorial fix in one chapter
Then propagate only if it improved the book. Don't apply notes wholesale.
2020 L8
Polish is LAST
Cut 10%, kill passive voice, upgrade verbs. Sentence work comes after structure.
2020 L8
Foreshadowing belongs in revision
"Remember a bucket" placeholders preserve momentum.
2025 L4
Flip the arc, not the plot
When something feels derivative, invert the character expectation. Plot is the hardest thing to innovate.
2025 L4
Short Fiction (Kowal)4 items
MICE Quotient
Milieu, Inquiry, Character, Event. How a thread OPENS determines how it must close.
2020 L7 (Kowal)
Nesting — last opened, first closed
Like nested function calls. Get the order wrong and you're holding a spare Allen wrench.
2020 L7 (Kowal)
The length formula
(characters + locations) × 750 × threads ÷ 1.5. Works for forecasting and for cutting.
2020 L7 (Kowal)
Red herrings are Inquiry threads
Which means they still need closing.
2020 L7 (Kowal)
Middle Grade / YA (Mull)3 items
MG/YA category rules
Age-based conventions for romance, violence and language. Sanderson covers none of this.
2016 L9 (Mull)
Category fit + a cool difference
His pitch test for middle grade.
2016 L9 (Mull)
Mull's cycle ≡ try-fail
Trouble → decisions → consequences, derived independently. The second convergence.
2016 L9 (Mull)
Publishing7 items · 1 unresolved
Your editor is your lifeline
No in-house champion and the book gets lost. Editors leaving mid-process is a real hazard.
2025 L10
The publisher's job isn't to make you money
It's to make them money. Sometimes those align.
2025 L10
Negotiate the breakpoint, not the rate
Royalty tiers move at thresholds. Corroborated across three lectures.
2020 L12
Advance size decides success
Identical sales read as triumph or failure depending on the advance. Real career consequences.
2020 L12
Keep film, foreign and world rights
Publishers have no film expertise. He calls non-competes morally reprehensible.
2025 L11
Ask what they bought recently
Not "what are you looking for." Everyone asks that.
2025 L11
Dollar figures are snapshots
Advances, rates and platforms drift visibly across his own retellings 2016→2020→2025.
